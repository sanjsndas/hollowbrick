    Design Principles: The Foundation of Good Space

    Great interior design and architecture are not about trends. They are about understanding how people move, rest, gather, and create. In this long-form editorial, we explore the enduring principles that separate a decorated room from a considered environment. From the golden ratio to the psychology of color, these are the tools that help designers make decisions that feel inevitable rather than arbitrary.

    A living space where proportion and material honesty create calm.

    Scale and Proportion: The Invisible Framework

    Before a single piece of furniture is placed, the bones of a room dictate its success. Ceiling height, window placement, and the relationship between rooms are architectural decisions that no amount of styling can override. We look at how to assess proportion and how to correct common imbalances—like a too-low ceiling or an overly long narrow room—through color, lighting, and furniture arrangement.

    Material Honesty and Patina

    Materials age. They scratch, fade, and develop character. The best interiors embrace this rather than fight it. We discuss why solid wood, natural stone, and unlacquered brass are often better long-term choices than their synthetic mimics, and how to design for the beauty of wear.

    This is not nostalgia; it's a practical approach to sustainability and longevity. When a material ages gracefully, you replace it less often. That's good for the planet and for your budget.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    How do I choose a color palette for a small room? +
    Start with a light, neutral base for walls and large furniture, then layer in one or two accent colors through textiles, art, and accessories. Avoid dark, saturated colors on all surfaces—they can make a space feel like a cave. Use the 60-30-10 rule: 60% dominant, 30% secondary, 10% accent.
    What is the best lighting for a home office? +
    Combine ambient light (ceiling fixture or recessed) with a dedicated task light (desk lamp) that you can adjust. Position the task light to avoid glare on your screen. If possible, place your desk perpendicular to a window so you get natural light without direct sun on your monitor.
    How can I make a rental feel like home? +
    Focus on what you can change: lighting (swap harsh fixtures for warm plug-in sconces), textiles (rugs, curtains, throws), and removable wallpaper or peel-and-stick tile. Invest in freestanding storage and art that you can take with you.
    What's the difference between interior design and interior decorating? +
    Interior design involves space planning, structural changes, building codes, and technical drawings. Interior decorating focuses on finishes, furniture, and styling within an existing layout. Both are valuable, but they require different skill sets.
